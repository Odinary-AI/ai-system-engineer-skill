# System Meaning and Decision Authority

Load this reference only when the mission's primary blocker is unresolved
meaning, semantic ownership, or accountable decision authority. Apply one
method unless a demonstrated dependency requires another.

## Active domain language

**Problem and expected effect.** Ambiguous, overloaded, or conflicting terms
can change a route, contract, behavior, test, or acceptance decision. This
method enables the next governed action to use one bounded meaning or exposes
the exact accountable decision that prevents it.

**Observable applicability.** Use when current sources apply materially
different meanings to the same term and at least one meaning changes action or
claim scope, normally across L0-L3. Do not use when wording is harmless,
explanatory vocabulary varies without changing a governed action, or current
authority already resolves the apparent conflict.

**Prerequisites, inputs, and resources.** Inspect user statements, the current
domain authority, specifications, code, tests, runtime facts, representative
scenarios, affected consumers, and the people accountable for domain truth.

**Logic and procedure.** Name each candidate meaning and source. Probe objects,
relationships, states, transformations, invariants, and edge cases through
concrete scenarios. Cross-check the candidates against current contracts, code,
tests, and runtime evidence. Propose the smallest precise distinction without
declaring it true; update an existing authority only after its owner decides.

**Result contract.** Return clarified candidate meanings, supporting and
conflicting facts, affected contracts and consumers, authority status, scope,
supported and unsupported conclusions, and the bounded pending decision. The
expected outcome is an unambiguous input for the named next action.

**Failure and honest degradation.** Missing authority, unrepresentative
scenarios, or code that conflicts with declared meaning can make resolution
unsafe. Do not invent a glossary owner or promote implementation behavior to
domain truth. Degrade to a sourced ambiguity map and name the smallest scenario
or accountable answer that would discriminate between meanings.

**Authority and claim boundary.** AI may identify conflicts, compare evidence,
and draft a candidate. The current authority or accountable owner establishes
domain truth. Generated evidence cannot prove acceptance or create
`human_confirmed`.

**Stop, handoff, and re-entry.** Stop when the next governed action can use one
meaning or one human-owned decision remains. Hand settled contract changes to
the ordinary workflow. Re-entry occurs when new evidence invalidates the
meaning, a consumer exposes another conflict, or the accountable decision
changes.

## Semantic ownership and confirmation-ready delta

**Problem and expected effect.** Changed or conflicting meaning can be placed
at the wrong semantic altitude or mixed with unchanged authority, causing a
lower layer to invent policy or a reviewer to reapprove everything. This method
enables one bounded accountable decision while preserving still-valid meaning.

**Observable applicability.** Use when a dependent action requires changed
meaning to be assigned to its owning layer and an actual human-owned decision
remains, typically L0-L3 with L4 evidence able to reopen it. Do not use when the
owner and meaning are already settled, the change is a reversible
implementation detail, or no decision changes action or claim scope.

**Prerequisites, inputs, and resources.** Use the binding project-local model
or L0-L4 coordinate, current authorities and confirmation sources, the proposed
change, affected consumers, evidence, decision owner, and dependent scope.

**Logic and procedure.** Split mixed statements by independently owned
meaning. Assign product purpose to L0, system responsibility to L1, operational
and domain contracts to L2, technical realization to L3, and observed facts to
L4. Reference unchanged authority. Present only new or reopened owning-layer
meaning as one addressable semantic delta, with one recommended answer and its
consequences. Separate decisions only when ownership or dependency differs.

**Result contract.** Return the owning layer or project-local equivalent,
still-valid authority, changed clauses, dependent scope, recommendation,
decision owner, supported facts, unsupported claims, and invalidation
conditions. The consumer receives a confirmation-ready delta rather than a
second authority.

**Failure and honest degradation.** A vague delta, mixed owners, missing source,
or disguised lower-layer design makes confirmation ambiguous. Do not multiply
requests by clause count or use polished wording to hide unresolved meaning.
Degrade to a draft classification and the single parent question that must be
answered before a decision can be requested.

**Authority and claim boundary.** AI may classify, trace, and recommend. Only
the accountable owner may confirm material meaning; one existing confirmation
may cover unchanged scope. Tests, reports, and generated evidence cannot create
`human_confirmed` or prove downstream implementation.

**Stop, handoff, and re-entry.** Stop when the bounded decision is recorded or
clearly pending and unaffected work is identified. Hand confirmed lower-layer
work to the ordinary workflow. Re-entry occurs when authority changes, evidence
contradicts the decision, or a downstream consumer reveals invented meaning;
reopen only the smallest owning scope.

## Product and system decision basis

**Problem and expected effect.** A material L2-L4 alternative may be
indistinguishable without a recurring product principle, red line,
responsibility boundary, or precedence rule. This method enables the affected
choice without creating a second product or system authority.

**Observable applicability.** Use when real alternatives cannot be decided
consistently from current L0-L1 authority and the missing basis could change
product or system meaning. Do not use when existing authority already
distinguishes the choice, or when the choice is ordinary, reversible, and
governed by settled criteria.

**Prerequisites, inputs, and resources.** Inspect current product and system
authority, alternatives, constraints, red lines and their sources, evidence,
responsible owners, deviation history, consumers, and any longer-horizon source
that might be mistaken for a current commitment.

**Logic and procedure.** Reuse an adequate current basis. Otherwise extract
only the principle, system boundary, non-negotiable red line, conflict
precedence, or bounded deviation rule needed to distinguish the alternatives.
Separate current commitments from research or future direction. Compare each
alternative against the proposed basis and return any changed L0 or L1 meaning
to its owner before choosing below it.

**Result contract.** Return the smallest candidate decision basis, its owner
and source, the alternatives it distinguishes, supported choice or pending
decision, local work that may continue, scope, unsupported conclusions, and
reopen conditions. The expected effect is a stable constraint for the named
lower-layer decision.

**Failure and honest degradation.** Abstract slogans, unsourced red lines,
conflicting principles, or missing ownership cannot govern a choice. Do not
create a second product brief, vision, architecture, template, or approval
workflow. Degrade to the alternatives, observed conflict, and one bounded
owner decision with the smallest evidence needed.

**Authority and claim boundary.** AI may draft, compare, and recommend the
basis. Accountable product or system owners retain decision authority. The
basis cannot prove feasibility, safety, implementation, release, product
outcome, acceptance, or `human_confirmed`.

**Stop, handoff, and re-entry.** Stop when affected alternatives are
distinguishable without invented upper meaning or when one accountable
decision remains. Hand the constrained choice to the ordinary workflow.
Re-entry occurs when a red line, owner, product outcome, system responsibility,
or material alternative changes or proves unsupported.
