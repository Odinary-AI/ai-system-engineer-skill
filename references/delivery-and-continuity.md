recovery, or absorption of a material correction. Ordinary planning and coding

## Vertical delivery slice

**Problem and expected effect.** A complex startup or material delivery path
may lack the smallest independently assessable operational outcome, causing
horizontal work, broad unfinished integration, or unsupported value claims.
This method enables a bounded vertical slice or walking skeleton.

**Observable applicability.** Use when ordinary delivery needs a smallest
actor-trigger-outcome scenario inside settled L0-L2 meaning, with L3 design and
L4 evidence still to follow. Do not use for a private repair, an isolated
enabler with no named outcome, or an already bounded coding task.

**Prerequisites, inputs, and resources.** Use current product, system, and
operational authority; actors, triggers, outcomes, state and authority changes;
success, failure, recovery, red lines, candidate slices, dependencies,
environments, evidence targets, and accountable selection policy.

**Logic and procedure.** Derive the selection envelope from L0-L2. Generate
only enough candidates to expose the real choice: smallest independent value,
walking skeleton through real integration and evidence, largest named-risk
retirement, or a prerequisite outcome that unlocks confirmed slices. Compare
outcome, acceptance, dependencies, reversibility, integration risk, failure,
recovery, and reopen conditions. Reject horizontal labels without independent
outcomes. AI selects only under a preconfirmed reversible policy; otherwise
return a small candidate set and one recommendation to the owner.

**Result contract.** Return slice identity and containing version, actor,
trigger, outcome, non-goals, authority sources, L3 responsibilities and seams,
dependencies, acceptance, integration and risk evidence, reusable and
invalidated work, owner, selection route, unsupported claims, and reopen
conditions. The consumer receives one independently assessable outcome.

**Failure and honest degradation.** A slice that hides several outcomes,
depends on broad unfinished layers, requires an unmade policy decision, or has
no observable acceptance is not independent. Do not rename a multi-feature
release or technical layer as a slice. Degrade to candidate comparison and the
smallest parent decision or integration evidence needed to select safely.

**Authority and claim boundary.** AI selects only inside confirmed policy and
reversible boundaries. Accountable humans own value, priority, authority,
contract, acceptance, red-line, and material-risk changes. Slice evidence
cannot prove version completion, release, product acceptance, or create
`human_confirmed`.

**Stop, handoff, and re-entry.** Stop when one slice is selected with a bounded
acceptance and evidence target. Hand implementation planning and coding to the
ordinary workflow. Re-entry occurs when the slice requires invented L0-L2
meaning, exposes a material dependency, invalidates selection policy, or a
higher-scope closure claim is requested.

## AI work unit and version scope

**Problem and expected effect.** AI delivery can conflate a coding work unit,
its parent vertical slice, and the containing version, causing local evidence
to be promoted into unsupported completion. This method enables a bounded work
handoff and scope-correct claims.

**Observable applicability.** Use when ordinary coding needs a bounded
objective, authority, scope, checks, dependencies, stop condition, and reopen
route, or when a completion claim risks crossing work-unit, slice, and version
objects. Do not use when the task and acceptance are already bounded and no
higher-scope claim is at issue. Typical affected layers are L2-L4, with version
trace to affected upper meaning only when the bounded claim depends on it. This
layer coordinate is not a fixed workflow; activate only affected layers.

**Prerequisites, inputs, and resources.** Use the selected slice and version
scope, current authority and design sources, changed and protected files or
interfaces, dependencies, integration consumer, acceptance criteria,
environments, required observations, current evidence, risks, and owners.

**Logic and procedure.** Define the smallest work unit worth an independent
test-and-review cycle. State objective, parent slice, allowed scope, non-goals,
inputs, dependencies, integration consumer, acceptance, checks, stop and
escalation. Keep one active owner for overlapping code, interfaces, schemas, or
data contracts. Parallelize only independent scope. Aggregate work-unit
evidence into a slice only after real integration and slice scenarios; aggregate
slices into a version only after affected cross-slice and operational evidence.
Only when an observable predicate is present—authentication, authorization,
personal or secret data, or an external dependency—the handoff requires
evidence for applicable trust boundaries, dependency authenticity and version,
exposure, failure behavior, and accountable human boundaries. Do not add this
branch when none of those predicates is observed.

**Result contract.** Return the work-unit handoff, parent slice and version,
authority, scope, dependencies, ownership, acceptance, evidence target,
implemented-result fields, deviations, supported work-unit claim, unsupported
slice or version claims, invalidation, stop, and reopen conditions.

**Failure and honest degradation.** Broad scope, hidden dependencies,
overlapping writers, accumulated unintegrated diffs, or local-only tests make a
higher claim unsafe. Do not treat task completion, code volume, or agent
agreement as integration. Degrade to the strongest supported object and name
the missing integration, scenario, cross-slice, or environment evidence.

**Authority and claim boundary.** AI may bound work inside settled meaning.
Work-unit, slice, version, installation, release, and product-acceptance claims
remain separate. Lower-scope tests cannot prove a higher object or create
`human_confirmed`.

**Stop, handoff, and re-entry.** Stop when ordinary coding can execute the
bounded handoff without guessing. Hand planning, TDD, debugging, review,
branching, integration, and test execution to the ordinary workflow. Re-entry
occurs for an upper-layer conflict, invalidated design, dependency or ownership
collision, irreversible consequence, or slice/version closure claim.

## Multi-version horizon

**Problem and expected effect.** Current design can either overbuild distant
versions or make an irreversible choice that blocks the next one. This method
enables current delivery while preserving only the future runway that can
materially change today's decision.

**Observable applicability.** Use when the next version, public contract, data
model, long-lead dependency, costly migration, or material assumption can
change a current design choice in one continuous product and system evolution
chain. Do not use when future detail cannot affect the current decision or the
named versions belong to different products or discontinuous systems.

**Prerequisites, inputs, and resources.** Use current L0-L4 meaning and
evidence, version outcomes and boundaries, next-horizon scenarios, longer-term
direction and red lines, public interfaces, data ownership, dependencies,
migration cost, critical assumptions, triggers, and accountable owners.

**Logic and procedure.** Design the current horizon for delivery. Design the
next only far enough to protect current choices: outcomes, key scenarios,
cross-layer effects, minimum runway, assumptions, and re-entry triggers. Keep
longer horizons as direction, red lines, assumptions, and triggers. Deepen only
for an irreversible or high-migration-cost decision, shared contract, long-lead
dependency, data model, operational risk, or prior validation that changes
current meaning. Reopen affected upper meaning rather than disguising a product
change as runway.

**Result contract.** Return current implementable scope, the minimum next
horizon protection, longer-term direction, red lines, assumptions, deferred
details, current decision impact, supported and unsupported horizon claims,
owners, and triggers. The expected effect is optionality without speculative
implementation design.

**Failure and honest degradation.** Fixed percentages, exhaustive roadmaps,
premature interfaces, or frozen long-range modules create false certainty.
Ignoring a shared irreversible boundary creates future rework. Degrade to
direction, the current red line, and one assumption or trigger whose evidence
can alter today's decision.

**Authority and claim boundary.** AI may recommend runway inside current
authority. Accountable owners govern product direction, system responsibility,
public commitments, and irreversible trade-offs. Future-horizon analysis does
not prove implementation, version completion, acceptance, or
`human_confirmed`.

**Stop, handoff, and re-entry.** Stop when further future detail would not
change current delivery. Hand the current bounded design to ordinary planning
and coding. Re-entry occurs when a trigger fires, an assumption is invalidated,
the next horizon becomes current, or future meaning changes today's product,
system, contract, or irreversible boundary.

## Recoverable handoff

**Problem and expected effect.** Work spanning contexts or agents can depend on
hidden conversation, obsolete reports, or unrouted decisions. This method
enables a fresh context to recover objective, authority, state, claims, next
action, and pending decisions from current sources.

**Observable applicability.** Use when work spans sessions or agents, hidden
conversation contains material state, or recovery cost is material across any
affected layer. Do not use for routine bounded work expected to finish in the
current context without material recovery risk.

**Prerequisites, inputs, and resources.** Use the current objective, scope and
non-goals, status, canonical authorities, confirmed decisions and sources,
specifications and plans, commits and diffs, evidence, failures, risks,
unsupported claims, pending human decisions, next action, and applicable
Skills. Remove secrets and personal data.

**Logic and procedure.** Maintain one current status pointer and one decision
trace. Reference rather than copy current sources. Distinguish completed,
in-progress, and not-started work; supported and unsupported claims; failures,
residual risks, invalidation and reopen conditions; stop reason, next smallest
action, and pending decisions. Give the entry to a fresh context and require it
to reconstruct state and challenge one assumption or disproportionate action.

**Result contract.** Return a self-contained recovery entry, current-source
pointers, decision trace, claim and evidence boundaries, next action, pending
decisions, and the fresh-context recovery result with any exposed gap. The
supported outcome is recoverability, not project correctness.

**Failure and honest degradation.** Competing ledgers, copied stale content,
hidden chat, missing source identity, or sensitive data make recovery unsafe.
Do not claim continuity from a document that no fresh context exercised.
Degrade to a gap report, last verified state, and the smallest source or owner
needed to restore recoverability.

**Authority and claim boundary.** A handoff points to current authority and
evidence; it replaces neither. Generated recovery evidence cannot prove product
correctness, acceptance, release, or create `human_confirmed`.

**Stop, handoff, and re-entry.** Stop when a fresh context can recover the
objective, state, authority, supported and unsupported claims, risks, next
action, and pending decisions. Hand execution to that context and its ordinary
workflow. Re-entry occurs when recovery fails, a pointer becomes stale, hidden
state reappears, or authority or evidence is invalidated.

## Material correction absorption

**Problem and expected effect.** A user or reviewer correction can expose a
failed reusable control and invalidate downstream claims; a local patch may
leave the failure able to recur. This method enables repair of the smallest
owner plus proportional prevention and fresh distinguishing evidence.

**Observable applicability.** Use when a correction changes a material
assumption, exposes a failed control, or invalidates dependent decisions,
actions, or claims. Do not use when the observation is harmless, local, and
reveals no failed control or material claim. The typical affected span starts at
the owning layer and includes only affected downstream scope. This layer
coordinate is not a fixed workflow; activate only affected layers.

**Prerequisites, inputs, and resources.** Use the correction and source,
original assumption, affected claims and consumers, admitting control, owning
authority or implementation, downstream scope, prior evidence, accountable
owner, and a realistic distinguishing check.

**Logic and procedure.** Capture the correction verbatim enough to preserve its
meaning. Classify the failed control and why it admitted the bad state. Repair
the smallest owning source and only dependent claims. Add prevention
proportional to recurrence and consequence. Freshly verify that the prevention
distinguishes the failed state from the repaired state; do not rely on the old
evidence it invalidated.

**Result contract.** Return corrected state, failed-control explanation,
affected and preserved scope, owner repair, proportional prevention, fresh
distinguishing evidence, supported and unsupported repaired claims, residual
limits, and expected recurrence reduction.

**Failure and honest degradation.** Treating every comment as a global rule,
repairing only a symptom, accumulating reviewers, or reusing invalidated tests
creates process without prevention. Degrade to a scoped correction, invalidated
claims, and the smallest check or owner decision needed before asserting
repair.

**Authority and claim boundary.** Reviewers and AI diagnose against accepted
contracts; they cannot silently redesign product or system meaning. The
accountable owner decides any changed normative meaning. Fresh tests support
only their scope and cannot create `human_confirmed`.

**Stop, handoff, and re-entry.** Stop when the affected claim is repaired and
fresh evidence distinguishes failed and repaired conditions, or when a
material trade-off has been returned to its owner. Hand ordinary changes to
the coding workflow. Re-entry occurs when prevention fails, another consumer
is affected, the correction changes upper meaning, or evidence is invalidated.
