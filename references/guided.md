# Guided Method Execution

Use this reference only after explicit Guided selection. `SKILL.md` is the sole
semantic authority: this file cannot override it, weaken its boundaries, or
create a second lifecycle. Select only methods whose observable trigger is
present. A method does not require a document, gate, agent, review, template,
or record merely because Guided is active.

## Shared methods

### Conditional altitude reasoning

**Use when:** Work crosses several system boundaries or a lower-layer choice
would otherwise guess higher-level meaning.

**Inputs and resources:** The requested outcome, current authorities, known
constraints, affected boundaries, evidence, and unresolved decisions.

**Run:** Start at the highest uncertain L0–L5 altitude. Resolve only the meaning
the next lower layer needs. At each descent, check sufficiency for the next
action and proportionality to the requested claim and risk.

**Return:** The bounded decisions, owners, constraints, and reopen conditions
needed by the next action.

**Failure and unsuitable cases:** Do not use for routine local work or expand
every altitude. A polished architecture artifact does not cure unresolved
authority.

**Authority, evidence, and handoff:** Return human-owned meaning to its
accountable owner. Record only decisions and evidence another context actually
needs.

**Stop:** Stop descending when the next layer can proceed without guessing and
more upper-layer design would not change the bounded decision or evidence.

### Product-design constitution

**Use when:** A material L2-L5 choice in a complex project would otherwise
invent a recurring cross-layer product principle, red line, or precedence.

**Inputs and resources:** Settled L0-L1 authorities, existing product
principles or equivalent authority, real alternatives, binding boundaries,
evidence, responsible owner, deviations, and any existing long-term vision or
research source.

**Run:** Reuse an adequate current authority. Otherwise draft only the meaning
needed to distinguish the affected alternatives: authority boundary,
actionable principles, sourced red lines, lexicographic conflict precedence,
and deviation scope, owner, recovery, expiry, and reopen condition. When a
long-term source exists, separate governing principles, implemented
capability, conditional exploration, and current non-commitment. Return any
change to product or system meaning to L0 or L1.

**Return:** The selected decision basis, owner-confirmed material meaning,
supported choice, local work that may continue, and reopen conditions.

**Failure and unsuitable cases:** Do not create a second PRD, architecture,
vision document, template, or approval workflow. Do not use for ordinary
reversible work with settled criteria or let principles prove implementation,
feasibility, safety, compliance, or acceptance.

**Authority, evidence, and handoff:** The accountable product owner confirms
principles, red lines, precedence, and material deviations. AI may draft and
compare but cannot create `human_confirmed`. Reference the current authority;
do not copy it into handoff.

**Stop:** Stop when the affected alternatives are distinguishable without
inventing product meaning. Do not complete unrelated future principles.

### Material decision interview

**Use when:** An unresolved human-owned product, architecture, authority,
lifecycle, or irreversible decision blocks an otherwise governed action.

**Inputs and resources:** Available facts, decision dependencies, alternatives,
recommendation, consequences, and work that does not depend on the decision.

**Run:** Inspect before asking. Resolve parent decisions before dependent ones.
Ask one decision question at a time, include a recommended answer and basis,
and continue unrelated reversible work.

**Return:** The accountable decision or a clearly pending boundary plus the
work that may safely continue.

**Failure and unsuitable cases:** Do not interview for local naming,
formatting, file placement, or other reversible implementation details already
governed by accepted contracts.

**Authority, evidence, and handoff:** AI may recommend but cannot create the
human decision. Preserve the decision source and affected claims.

**Stop:** Stop asking when the blocking parent decision is resolved or the
accountable user must decide before dependent implementation continues.

### Existing behavior change

**Use when:** A refactor, migration, or fix may change behavior relied on by a
known consumer.

**Inputs and resources:** Observed public behavior, consumers, realistic
examples, affected tests, failure behavior, and the intended change.

**Run:** Characterize the observed contract before changing internals, then
invoke TDD for the bounded behavior. Use the dedicated development Skills for
their execution algorithms.

**Return:** The preserved or intentionally changed behavior and affected test
evidence.

**Failure and unsuitable cases:** Do not freeze accidental internals or infer
product truth from legacy behavior. Routine fixes with an established contract
do not require ASE governance.

**Authority, evidence, and handoff:** Identify whether observed behavior,
accepted specification, or an accountable decision governs each difference.

**Stop:** Stop when the named consumer contract is preserved or the approved
change and affected realistic checks pass.

### Predecessor convergence

**Use when:** Old and target systems, compatibility, hidden consumers, or
retirement affect current authority or irreversible change.

**Inputs and resources:** Semantic inventory, consumers, predecessor evidence,
target contracts, environments, compatibility owner, and recovery options.

**Run:** Compare meaning rather than file shape. Trace consumers, move
canonical ownership deliberately, give compatibility a named consumer and
exit condition, and verify replacement before retirement.

**Return:** Retained, transformed, replaced, or retired semantics with consumer
and residual-risk dispositions.

**Failure and unsuitable cases:** Predecessor behavior is evidence, not an
oracle. Do not preserve unused compatibility or delete on target-only tests.

**Authority, evidence, and handoff:** Keep source, environment, freshness, and
claim limits attached to predecessor and replacement evidence.

**Stop:** Stop when every in-scope semantic and consumer has a supported
disposition; irreversible retirement still requires recovery evidence and
accountable confirmation.

### Package and claim objects

**Use when:** Work makes a source, package, installation, tag, publication, or
release claim.

**Inputs and resources:** Exact source identity, package manifest, target
environment, install result, tag or publication state, and requested claim.

**Run:** Treat every object as separate. Use exact mirroring for a copy and a
real-environment smoke for the installed target. Validate only objects actually
exercised.

**Return:** Supported and unsupported claim objects with evidence,
environment, and invalidation conditions.

**Failure and unsuitable cases:** Source tests do not prove installation,
publication, release, product outcome, or acceptance.

**Authority, evidence, and handoff:** Preserve publication and release
permission boundaries; only an accountable human can create
`human_confirmed`.

**Stop:** Stop at the strongest requested claim object supported by current
evidence.

### Domain quality

**Use when:** Subjective artifact quality, expert domain judgment, or
misleading metrics materially affects the claim.

**Inputs and resources:** Real artifacts, representative cases, relevant
external or predecessor floors, qualified review criteria, and known failures.

**Run:** Separate generator from critic, inspect real outputs, compare against
credible reference floors, and distinguish runtime repair from durable system
learning.

**Return:** A bounded quality finding, actionable failures, evidence limits,
and residual uncertainty.

**Failure and unsuitable cases:** Structural completeness, self-critique, or
model agreement alone cannot prove domain quality or human acceptance.

**Authority, evidence, and handoff:** Keep qualified human judgment and
generated findings distinct; hand off cases and criteria rather than a
manufactured score.

**Stop:** Stop when the requested quality claim has the necessary real-artifact
and review evidence, or state why it remains unsupported.

## Selective v3.1 methods

### Active domain language

**Use when:** Ambiguous, overloaded, or conflicting terms could change a
system route, contract, behavior, test, or acceptance decision.

**Inputs and resources:** User statements, the project-designated current
domain authority if one exists, specifications, code, tests, runtime facts,
and representative scenarios.

**Run:** Name each conflicting meaning and source. Propose a precise candidate
term without declaring it true. Probe objects, relationships, states,
transformations, invariants, and edge cases with concrete scenarios.
Cross-check the candidate against code and runtime behavior. After the required
confirmation, update only the existing designated authority. Suggest an
architecture decision record only when the choice is hard to reverse,
surprising without context, and the result of a real trade-off.

**Return:** The clarified meanings, supporting or conflicting facts, affected
contracts or tests, authority status, and any still-pending decision.

**Failure and unsuitable cases:** Do not activate for harmless wording, invent
a glossary owner, force a document system, or treat explanatory vocabulary as
operational when it changes no route, contract, code, test, or acceptance.

**Authority, evidence, and handoff:** An AI term is a proposal, not domain
truth. Reference the existing authority and confirmation source; if none
exists, keep the ambiguity explicit or escalate only at a material accountable
boundary.

**Stop:** Stop when the next governed action can use one unambiguous meaning,
or when an explicit human-owned domain decision blocks it.

### L4 deep modules

**Use when:** Designing or materially changing a module boundary, public
interface, dependency seam, adapter, or module-level test strategy.

**Inputs and resources:** Responsibilities, callers, public obligations,
invariants, failure modes, lifecycle and authority owners, dependencies,
change history, implementations, and realistic behaviors.

**Run:** Seek leverage and locality: hide meaningful behavior behind a narrow,
stable interface. Place a seam only where responsibility, lifecycle,
authority, failure, change, an unstable external dependency, or an independent
test boundary genuinely varies. Keep dependency and call direction explicit.
Let adapters translate protocol or representation without becoming a business
fact owner. Test behavior through the interface callers use. Apply the
deletion test: if removing the proposed module removes only forwarding, keep
the behavior together; if complexity spreads across callers, the boundary may
be earning its place.

**Return:** The chosen responsibility, interface obligations, seam and adapter
roles, dependency direction, public behavioral evidence, and rejected shallow
splits.

**Failure and unsuitable cases:** Do not split because smaller files look
cleaner, create a seam for a hypothetical variation, expose internals for
testing, or optimize implementation-line ratios.

**Authority, evidence, and handoff:** L4 cannot invent unresolved L0–L3
meaning. Adapters do not own domain facts. Hand off public contracts and
failure boundaries, not internal layout as product truth.

**Stop:** Stop when callers can use and test the interface without hidden
coupling, changes stay local, and further splitting would add forwarding or
cognitive cost without a real boundary.

### Evidence-routed prototype

**Use when:** A design-changing unknown cannot be answered from current facts
or dialogue, and either the user must see alternatives to recognize a
preference or executable behavior is required.

**Inputs and resources:** One explicit unknown, its design impact, existing
evidence, user availability, the cheapest suitable medium, disposal boundary,
and the separate `find-unknown` capability when available.

**Run:** First invoke `find-unknown` to choose the evidence route; do not
silently bypass it. If it selects a prototype, state one question and build
only the cheapest runnable or visible artifact sufficient to answer it. Mark
it disposable before work starts, avoid production hardening, expose the
relevant state or alternatives, and observe the user's reaction or behavior.
If the separate capability is unavailable, name that gap before proposing the
same smallest validation without copying its full routing method.

**Return:** Observations, bounded conclusion, limitations, invalidation
conditions, and the resulting design impact. Retain a pointer only when useful;
the prototype code is not the design authority.

**Failure and unsuitable cases:** Do not prototype facts available from source
inspection, ask abstract preference questions that can be shown cheaply, solve
several questions at once, or turn runnable output into production code by
default.

**Authority, evidence, and handoff:** A prototype provides evidence for its one
question. It does not establish production quality, stakeholder acceptance,
release readiness, or a human decision without the required authority.

**Stop:** Stop as soon as the question is answered well enough to update the
design, or when the medium cannot produce discriminating evidence and a new
validation route is needed.

### Recoverable handoff

**Use when:** Work spans sessions or agents, recovery cost is material, or a
fresh context must continue without relying on hidden conversation.

**Inputs and resources:** Current objective, scope and non-goals, current
control state, canonical authorities, confirmed decisions and sources,
current specs and plans, commits and diffs, evidence, failures, risks, pending
human decisions, and next applicable Skills.

**Run:** Maintain one current status pointer and one decision trace. Reference
rather than copy current sources. Record completed, in-progress, and not-started
work; supported and unsupported claims; failures, residual risks, reopen
conditions, stop reason, next smallest action, pending decisions, and suggested
next Skills. Remove secrets, credentials, personal data, and unnecessary
environment output. Give the result to a fresh context and require it to
recover the state and challenge one assumption or disproportionate action.

**Return:** A self-contained recovery entry plus the fresh-context recovery
result and any exposed gap.

**Failure and unsuitable cases:** Do not create a competing status ledger,
copy whole specs or diffs, trust obsolete reports, include sensitive data, or
require a handoff for routine bounded work that will finish in the current
context.

**Authority, evidence, and handoff:** The handoff points to authorities; it is
not one. Generated recovery evidence cannot create human confirmation or prove
product correctness.

**Stop:** Stop when a fresh context can recover objective, state, authority,
claims, risks, next action, and pending decisions from current sources; report
recoverability only.

## Failure recovery methods

### Material correction

**Use when:** A user or reviewer correction reveals a failed control rather
than a harmless local observation.

**Inputs and resources:** Corrected assumption, affected claims, admitting
control, owning source, downstream consumers, and a distinguishing test.

**Run:** Capture the correction, classify the failed control, repair the
smallest owner and affected claims, add only proportional prevention, and
freshly verify that the prevention distinguishes failed and repaired states.

**Return:** Corrected state, failed-control explanation, prevention, fresh
evidence, and remaining limits.

**Failure and unsuitable cases:** Do not turn every observation into a global
rule or accumulate reviewers after the affected claim is freshly checked.

**Authority, evidence, and handoff:** A reviewer diagnoses against the accepted
contract and does not silently redesign product meaning.

**Stop:** Stop when the affected claim is repaired and the prevention is
distinguishing, or return a material trade-off to its owner.

### Irreversible retirement

**Use when:** Deletion, retirement, destructive migration, or loss of recovery
would be difficult to reverse.

**Inputs and resources:** Replacement evidence, semantic and consumer
inventory, recovery boundary, residual risks, and accountable decision.

**Run:** Verify replacement in the relevant environment, disposition consumers
and semantics, preserve recovery until the decision boundary is crossed, and
separate reversible preparation from irreversible execution.

**Return:** Supported preparation, missing evidence, recovery state, and the
pending or confirmed retirement decision.

**Failure and unsuitable cases:** Passing target tests alone does not prove
replacement, zero consumers, safe deletion, or authority to retire.

**Authority, evidence, and handoff:** Only the accountable owner authorizes the
irreversible step; generated evidence cannot substitute.

**Stop:** Stop before destruction unless replacement, disposition, recovery,
and accountable confirmation are all present.
